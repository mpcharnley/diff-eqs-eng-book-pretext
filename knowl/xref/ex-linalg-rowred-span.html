Exercise 23.

Consider the vectors
\begin{equation*} \vec{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} \quad \vec{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 5 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Let \(A\) be the matrix with these vectors as columns and \(\vec{b}\) the vector \([1\ 0 \ 0]\text{.}\)
🔗

(a)

Compute the rank of \(A\) to determine how many of these vectors are linearly independent.
🔗
Answer.
The rank is 2.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

Determine if \(\vec{b}\) is in the span of the given vectors by using row reduction to try to solve \(A\vec{x} = \vec{b}\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
No, it is not in the span.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(c)

Look at the columns of the row-reduced form of \(A\text{.}\) Is \(\vec{b}\) in the span of those vectors?
🔗
Answer.
Yes, it is in the span, because the first vector is exactly $\vec{b}$.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(d)

What do these last two parts tell you about the span of the columns of a matrix, and the span of the columns of the row-reduced matrix?
🔗
Answer.
This says that these two spans are not the same. We can not use the row-reduced matrix in order to figure out if something is in the span. We need to use the pivot columns to go back to the original vectors to simplify the span.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(e)

Now, build a matrix \(D\) with these vectors as rows. Row-reduce this matrix to get a matrix \(D_2\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\(D_2 = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1/2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right] \)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(f)

Is \(\vec{b}\) in the span of the rows of \(D_2\text{?}\) You can’t check this in using the matrix form; instead, just brute force it based on the form of \(D_2\text{.}\) What does this potentially say about the span of the rows of \(D\) and the rows of \(D_2\text{?}\)
🔗
Answer.
No, it is not. If we add the two rows together, we get \([1\ 0 \ -1/2]\) and we have no way to cancel out that last term. This suggests that we can use either the rows of the original matrix or the rows of the row-reduced form in order to work out the span of the rows.
